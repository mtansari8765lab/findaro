AI Tools Directory

25 well-known AI tools, described from their published features. Pricing is stated in general terms only — always confirm current plans on the official site.

Dr.Find lists well-known, currently available AI tools that a reader can verify on the official website linked from each listing. A product is included because it is widely used and clearly documented — not because anyone paid for placement. Dr.Find has no affiliate relationships with the tools listed as of October 2026; links go to official websites. If affiliate links are ever introduced, they will be labeled. Pricing is described only in general terms — whether a free tier is available and whether paid plans exist — because prices change; always confirm current plans on the official site. Listings are directory entries based on published features, not hands-on testing, benchmarks, or scores. Dr.Find does not claim to have tested these tools.

Listings are grouped by category: AI Assistants & Chatbots, AI Search, Writing, Image, Video, Audio & Voice, Coding, Productivity & Notes, and Meeting & Transcription. Each listing was last reviewed in October 2026; if a listing is wrong or outdated, please report it to mtansari8765@gmail.com.

ChatGPT

AI Assistants & Chatbots · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

OpenAI's conversational AI assistant. It answers questions, drafts and edits text, helps with ideas, summarises documents, and can generate images via DALL·E in supported plans. Widely used for writing help, study support, coding assistance and everyday problem-solving.

Best for: Everyday questions, drafting and brainstorming

Strengths:

  • Very broad general knowledge and strong conversational ability
  • Large ecosystem of custom GPTs and integrations
  • Voice and image input in supported versions

Limitations:

  • Can produce confident but incorrect answers — verify important facts
  • Free tier has usage limits and may lack the newest models
  • Quality varies by plan and model version

Claude

AI Assistants & Chatbots · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

Anthropic's AI assistant, known for long-context document work, nuanced writing and coding help. Commonly used to summarise and discuss long documents, draft careful prose, and work through analysis and reasoning tasks.

Best for: Long documents, careful writing and analysis

Strengths:

  • Strong at long documents and sustained, coherent writing
  • Often praised for careful, nuanced tone
  • Useful for document Q&A and structured analysis

Limitations:

  • Free tier has message limits
  • Availability and features vary by country and plan
  • Like all chatbots, it can still make factual errors

Google Gemini

AI Assistants & Chatbots · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

Google's AI assistant, integrated across Google services including Search, Gmail, Docs and Android in supported regions and plans. Used for drafting, summarising, planning and multimodal tasks combining text and images.

Best for: Google users who want AI inside familiar apps

Strengths:

  • Deep integration with Google apps and services
  • Strong multimodal (text + image) handling
  • Familiar Google account sign-in

Limitations:

  • Feature availability differs a lot by region and plan
  • Answers can still be inaccurate — check sources
  • Some integrations require a paid Google plan

Microsoft Copilot

AI Assistants & Chatbots · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

Microsoft's AI assistant, built into Windows, Edge, Bing and Microsoft 365 apps in supported plans. Used for drafting in Word and Outlook, summarising meetings and documents, and everyday web-assisted questions.

Best for: Windows and Microsoft 365 users

Strengths:

  • Built into Windows, Edge and Microsoft 365 workflows
  • Web-connected answers via Bing in supported modes
  • Familiar for Office users

Limitations:

  • Best features need a Microsoft 365 subscription
  • Experience varies between consumer and work versions
  • Still requires fact-checking for important claims

Poe

AI Assistants & Chatbots · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

Quora's multi-model chat platform. Poe lets you talk to bots powered by different AI models — including models from OpenAI, Anthropic and others — in one interface, and create simple custom bots.

Best for: Trying several AI models in one place

Strengths:

  • Access to many models from one account
  • Easy way to compare how models answer the same prompt
  • Custom bot creation without coding

Limitations:

  • Free usage is limited by a points/compute system
  • You are using models through an intermediary, not directly
  • Bot quality varies widely

Character.AI

AI Assistants & Chatbots · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

A platform for chatting with AI characters — fictional, historical-style, or user-created personas. Primarily entertainment and creative roleplay rather than a factual research or work tool.

Best for: Entertainment chat and roleplay characters

Strengths:

  • Huge library of user-created characters
  • Engaging for creative writing and roleplay
  • Free to start chatting

Limitations:

  • Not designed for factual accuracy — treat answers as fiction
  • Free tier can have queues and limits
  • Content and safety filters apply

Perplexity

AI Search · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

An AI answer engine that responds to questions with a written answer plus numbered citations to web sources you can open and check. Popular for quick research, summaries of current topics, and follow-up questions.

Best for: Research questions with visible sources

Strengths:

  • Answers include citations you can click through to verify
  • Good for current-events and research-style questions
  • Clean, focused reading experience

Limitations:

  • Citations do not guarantee the answer interpreted them correctly
  • Free tier limits the most capable searches
  • Always open the source for important decisions

Brave Search (AI answers)

AI Search · Free to use; see official site for any current plans

Brave's independent search engine, with an AI answer feature (Answer with AI / Leo integration) that summarises results at the top of the page alongside conventional links. Brave emphasises an independent index and privacy.

Best for: Privacy-conscious search with optional AI summaries

Strengths:

  • Independent search index, not just a reskin of Google or Bing
  • AI summaries sit alongside normal results you can inspect
  • Privacy-focused positioning

Limitations:

  • Index can be thinner for very niche local queries
  • AI summaries carry the usual accuracy caveats
  • Interface differs from what Google users expect

Grammarly

Writing · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

A writing assistant that checks grammar, spelling, punctuation and clarity in browsers, documents and apps, with AI features for rewriting and drafting in supported plans. Widely used by students and professionals for everyday writing polish.

Best for: Grammar, spelling and tone while you write

Strengths:

  • Works almost everywhere you type via browser extension and apps
  • Clear explanations, not just corrections
  • Tone and clarity suggestions help non-native writers

Limitations:

  • Best features are behind the paid plan
  • Suggestions can flatten personal style if accepted blindly
  • Cloud processing means text is sent to Grammarly's service

QuillBot

Writing · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

A paraphrasing and writing toolkit: reword sentences in different tones, summarise text, check grammar, and generate citations. Popular with students for reworking drafts — though paraphrasing must still respect academic integrity rules.

Best for: Paraphrasing and rewording text

Strengths:

  • Multiple paraphrase modes (formal, simple, fluency and more)
  • Bundles summariser, grammar checker and citation tools
  • Generous free starting point for short texts

Limitations:

  • Free tier limits word counts and modes
  • Over-reliance can breach academic integrity policies
  • Paraphrases still need a human read-through

DeepL

Writing · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

A translation service widely praised for natural-sounding results, particularly among European languages, plus DeepL Write for improving English and German text. Used by businesses and individuals for documents, emails and web text.

Best for: High-quality translation, especially European languages

Strengths:

  • Translations often read more naturally than word-for-word tools
  • Document translation preserves formatting in supported plans
  • Write feature helps polish tone and style

Limitations:

  • Supports fewer languages than Google Translate
  • Free tier limits characters and document use
  • Nuance can still be lost in idioms and legal text

Canva (Magic Studio)

Image · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

Canva is a drag-and-drop design platform; Magic Studio is its suite of AI features, including text-to-image generation, background removal, Magic Write and design suggestions, woven into templates for social posts, presentations and documents.

Best for: Non-designers making posts, slides and graphics

Strengths:

  • Extremely approachable for non-designers
  • AI features live inside a complete design workflow
  • Huge template and stock library

Limitations:

  • Many AI features and assets need a paid plan
  • AI generation has usage limits
  • Fine control is limited compared with professional design software

Midjourney

Image · Paid plans — see official site for current pricing; no ongoing free tier as of this review

An AI image generator known for striking, artistic results, accessed via its website and Discord. Popular with illustrators, marketers and hobbyists for concept art, mood imagery and stylised visuals.

Best for: Artistic, high-aesthetic image generation

Strengths:

  • Distinctive, high-quality artistic style
  • Active community and lots of shared prompt inspiration
  • Strong at atmospheric and stylised imagery

Limitations:

  • No ongoing free tier — a subscription is required
  • Precise control (exact text, layouts) can be tricky
  • Commercial terms depend on your plan — check them

DALL·E (via ChatGPT)

Image · Available through ChatGPT; free access is limited where offered — see official site for current pricing

OpenAI's image generation model, accessed through ChatGPT: describe an image in conversation and refine it with follow-up messages. Convenient for illustrations, mock-ups and concept images without learning a separate tool.

Best for: Generating images conversationally inside ChatGPT

Strengths:

  • Conversational editing — refine images by describing changes
  • No separate account or interface to learn
  • Good at following detailed scene descriptions

Limitations:

  • Availability and limits depend on your ChatGPT plan
  • Fine-grained control is less direct than dedicated editors
  • Generated images still need checking for errors (hands, text, logos)

Leonardo AI

Image · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

An AI image platform aimed at creators and game development, with fine-tuned models for styles like illustration and photorealism, plus canvas editing and texture tools. Popular for game assets and consistent-style artwork.

Best for: Game assets, illustrations and controlled image creation

Strengths:

  • Style-specific models give more control than general generators
  • Free tier lets you experiment before paying
  • Useful editing and upscaling tools in one place

Limitations:

  • Free tier uses a token system that runs out
  • Interface has more options to learn
  • Output rights depend on plan — check the terms

Runway

Video · Free tier/credits available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

A creative AI platform best known for text-to-video and image-to-video generation (Gen models) plus editing tools like background removal and motion tracking. Used by filmmakers, marketers and social creators for short AI-assisted clips.

Best for: AI video generation and editing for creators

Strengths:

  • Among the most capable AI video generation tools
  • Combines generation with practical editing tools
  • Web-based — no powerful local hardware needed

Limitations:

  • Video generation consumes credits quickly
  • Clips are short; long-form still needs traditional editing
  • Results can be unpredictable and need several attempts

ElevenLabs

Audio & Voice · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

An AI voice platform offering text-to-speech in many languages, voice cloning (with consent requirements), and dubbing tools. Used for narration, audiobooks, videos, accessibility and prototypes.

Best for: Realistic text-to-speech and voice narration

Strengths:

  • Among the most natural-sounding AI voices
  • Many languages and voice styles
  • API available for developers

Limitations:

  • Free tier is limited in characters
  • Voice cloning raises consent and misuse concerns — only clone voices you have rights to
  • Long-form narration still benefits from human review

Suno

Audio & Voice · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

An AI music generator that turns a text prompt — style, mood, topic — into a song with vocals and instrumentation. Used for fun, demos, social content and songwriting inspiration.

Best for: Generating songs from a text description

Strengths:

  • Creates complete songs, vocals included, in minutes
  • No musical training needed to get started
  • Good for demos and creative inspiration

Limitations:

  • Free creations are typically for non-commercial use — check terms
  • Limited control over exact composition
  • Quality and coherence vary between generations

Notion (with Notion AI)

Productivity & Notes · Free tier available; paid plans and AI add-on — see official site for current pricing

A flexible notes and workspace app combining documents, wikis, simple databases and project boards. Notion AI adds summarising, drafting, translation and Q&A over your workspace in supported plans.

Best for: Notes, docs and team wikis in one workspace

Strengths:

  • One tool replaces separate notes, wiki and lightweight project apps
  • Highly customisable pages and templates
  • AI can search and summarise your own notes

Limitations:

  • Can become disorganised without a system
  • AI features typically cost extra
  • Offline support is more limited than some rivals

Otter.ai

Meeting & Transcription · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

A meeting assistant that records, transcribes and summarises conversations, including joining Zoom, Teams and Google Meet calls in supported plans. Used for interviews, lectures and team meetings.

Best for: Automatic meeting transcription and summaries

Strengths:

  • Searchable transcripts save hours of note-taking
  • Speaker labels and summaries help follow up
  • Live transcription during meetings

Limitations:

  • Accuracy drops with strong accents, crosstalk and poor audio
  • Free tier caps transcription minutes
  • Recording meetings requires participants' consent — check local rules

Fireflies.ai

Meeting & Transcription · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

An AI meeting assistant that transcribes calls across major video platforms, generates summaries and action items, and pushes notes into tools like CRMs and project apps in supported plans.

Best for: Meeting notes that sync to your CRM and tools

Strengths:

  • Wide range of integrations (CRM, Slack, project tools)
  • Search across all your past meetings
  • Action items and topic trackers

Limitations:

  • Like all transcription, errors occur — review key quotes
  • Privacy and consent policies matter for recorded calls
  • Best value is for teams, less so for occasional users

GitHub Copilot

Coding · Free tier available for eligible users; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

GitHub's AI pair programmer: suggests code completions and functions in editors like VS Code, offers chat help, and assists with explanations and tests. Trained positioning is as an assistant — you remain responsible for reviewing its code.

Best for: AI code completion inside your editor

Strengths:

  • Deeply integrated into popular editors and GitHub
  • Speeds up boilerplate and routine coding
  • Chat can explain unfamiliar code

Limitations:

  • Suggestions can be wrong or insecure — review everything
  • Free tier has request limits
  • Licensing and training-data questions remain debated

Cursor

Coding · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

An AI-focused code editor built on a VS Code-style interface, designed around chatting with your codebase, multi-file edits and autocomplete powered by current AI models. Popular with developers who want AI woven through the whole editor.

Best for: An AI-first code editor for projects

Strengths:

  • Understands project context across files
  • Familiar VS Code-style interface and extensions
  • Strong multi-file editing workflow

Limitations:

  • Another subscription for heavy use
  • AI edits still need careful review and testing
  • Switching editors has a learning cost

Replit

Coding · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

A browser-based development platform where you can write, run and host apps without local setup, with AI assistance (Replit AI / Agent features) that can help generate and modify projects, especially for beginners and quick prototypes.

Best for: Building and hosting small apps from the browser

Strengths:

  • Zero setup — code and deploy from the browser
  • Great for learning and quick prototypes
  • Collaboration is built in

Limitations:

  • Free tier limits resources and always-on hosting
  • Less suited to large, complex production systems
  • AI-generated projects still need review for security

Zapier

Productivity & Notes · Free tier available; paid plans — see official site for current pricing

An automation platform connecting thousands of apps: when something happens in one app (a new form entry, email or order), Zapier can act in another (add a row, send a message, create a task). Increasingly includes AI steps and chatbots.

Best for: Automating repetitive tasks between apps

Strengths:

  • Huge app catalogue — most tools you use are supported
  • No code needed for common automations
  • Reliable for small-business workflows

Limitations:

  • Free tier limits tasks and update speed
  • Costs grow with automation volume
  • Complex workflows can be hard to debug
